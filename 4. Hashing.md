## Hashing

- Prove that you cannot find(k) faster than O log n
- Show how to find(k) faster than O log n

Comparison model to find items
Given a key and an item or two items, I can compare the keys, I don't
look at what the keys hold

=, <, >, >=, <=, not equal

Direct Access Array
Container: build(A) - u (largest integer)
Static: find(k) - 1
Dynamic: insert(k) and delete(k) - 1
Order: find_min(), find_max() - u
find_prev(k), find_next(k) - u

Only when u < 2 power w - the size of a word
of memory. w is the word size of your machine, the number of words
a machine can work on

We are using too much space when we have a large u

====
Solution: Instead of allocating alot of space u, allocate
less space, eg. store in length m = O (n)
Then map the large space of keys, down to 0 to m-1

Then instead of storing at an index, store more
than one thing at an index position
We want a hash function that will evenly distribute items, over the keys

Dealing with collisions
Point the items to another data structure, and keep them
short through chaining

How do we pick a good hash function
- Note, as long as u is large than n, there will be a collision

What is the easiest think to do to get down from
a large u to n

Division hash function is the answe
h(k) = k modulus m as long as the keys are evenly distributed
